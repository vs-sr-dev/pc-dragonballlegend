# Curiosities

## The Japanese team's afterword (`TF_DBZ.DOC`)

The European disc still carries a text file from the Japanese one:
「ドラゴンボールＺ 偉大なるドラゴンボール伝説　セガサターン版　開発後記
presented by Project ＤＢＺ」, the Saturn version's development
afterword, in Shift-JIS, 429 lines. It thanks the buyer and passes on
"a message from the Z fighters": a note from each member of the team.
Dated in it: 96/03/09 and "3/20 (Wed), after an all-nighter" (20 March
1996 was a Wednesday), the weeks before the Japanese master. What it
says about the game and its making (paraphrased from the Japanese):

* **Bandai's "Dragon Suzuki"** opens: many system changes, characters
  redrawn several times.
* **Producer Hirofumi Inagaki**: game designer Suzuki ("Beck") asked
  for an evolution of the PC Engine game (*Idainaru Son Gokū Densetsu*)
  where several characters fight on one field and the player swaps
  between them. At first the player gave orders with buttons, as on the
  PC Engine; after several reviews the present system came in December.
  The PlayStation and Saturn masters overlapped (the PlayStation one was
  not finished yet): "the hardest job of all the products I have had".
* **Composer Kenji Yamamoto**: his eighth Dragon Ball Z score. The Super
  Famicom gave 8 voices with the effects, the Saturn and the PlayStation
  24; the theme was "avoid bright music as far as possible", "it should
  tingle", "mechanical": house music with guitars throughout. Thanks to
  the sound programmer, Yamada, who ported his music and tone data.
* **The main programmer, Kanazawa**, his first time as lead, lists his
  year: his dog died, his uncle died on New Year's morning, he married
  (the uncle was to be the go-between, so the betrothal kept moving), his
  wife was hospitalised, the work grew several times over, the release
  slipped twice, he moved into the office, and his first son was born
  before the master ("I have seen him five times in three weeks").
  Under "endless bugs": *"A bug came up." "That's the spec."* His
  glossary: the "Yamada incident", the PlayStation version's main
  programmer vanishing, which delayed the release and so lengthened
  this team's schedule.
* **"The legendary wit battle"**: a counter of CONNECT hits written in
  ten minutes drew numbers flying from each fighter's head; it had one
  digit, so above 9 it drew whatever followed in memory: unidentifiable
  things, a tree, a gauge's face. "The theme has changed", said the
  staff, and named the third stage "Frieza Quakes! A Fierce Battle of
  Wits!".
* **Programmer Saeki**, after an all-nighter, sings nonsense; an
  anonymous programmer "K" is moved to have made his first Saturn game.
* **Programmer "Kaiser"** wrote the chain hits in two months, his tenth
  game: here he could direct the camera "as if I were filming".
* **"MK"** did part of the chain hits and the characters' entrance
  demos: the fighters are hand-drawn sprites, not polygons.
* **"Nabeo"** drew the polygon terrain and the backgrounds.
* **Graphic designer "N"**: the game inherits the PC Engine
  *Idainaru Son Gokū Densetsu*, "the second of the Playing Animation
  series". More than 30 chain-battle characters, including ones no
  fighting game had used (Nappa, Guldo, Jeice, Burter, Androids 17 and
  19). Planned but cut for time: every form of Frieza (traces remain in
  the entrance demo), Cell and Super Buu, the Saibamen (they became a
  chain hit), Pui Pui, Yakon, Yamcha, Tien ("I regret leaving him
  out"), Chiaotzu, Gotenks, Gogeta, Ozotto, and an original character
  designed by Toriyama; special abilities in battle (Goku's instant
  transmission, the androids' energy absorption, regeneration) and
  bigger sprites.
* **"Doppy"** (effects, the mode screens): the POWER BALANCE gauge
  changed in every magazine preview; **the option screen's wallpaper is
  a scan of the office floor** they worked on.
* **"Graphicer-A"** (the auto-battle characters and the chain hits'
  sprites) feared polygons and anime sprites would not match; thinks
  they match better on the Saturn than on the PlayStation ("but the ki
  effects are prettier on the PS").
* **Sound, Yamada**: "the pictures move and spin, and it plays about
  2.5 hours of dialogue in time while playing the music and the
  effects". **Sound, "Yagi"**: more than 1 500 lines by the voice
  actors, the effects taken from the anime; "and there seems to be a
  ○○○○ mode" (a hidden mode, its name masked).
* **Game designer Hisayasu Suzuki** closes: two teams, two machines at
  once, "the main programmer really ran away (PS version)", the 1 500
  lines the hardest part. The idea came in autumn 1994, dozing on the
  Inokashira line: "let's throw Goku and the others into a pot and play".
  He quotes Toriyama, in the *Daizenshū* interview, saying the heroes
  are many and the villain alone, "a bit cowardly, when you think of
  it": "Cowardly is fine!! … if you think it's cowardly, fight one on
  one".

## The Japanese logo in a French game

The opening movie (`MV_OPD`) ends on the Japanese logo,
偉大なるドラゴンボール伝説; the title screen keeps a small
「ドラゴンボール」 under the French title. Only the text that the
programs draw was translated.

## A French typo on the title

The title screen asks `APPUIES SUR START` (the imperative is
*appuie*): as on the disc, not a dump's fault.

## "Chergemant..."

The picture shown while a battle loads (Vegeta and Nappa over a burst
of speed lines) has its word drawn into it, misspelt: `Chergemant...`
for *Chargement...* (seen by the user in the runtime, session 2). With
`APPUIES SUR START` on the title, the French release's lettering had
no proofreader.

## Tracks 10 to 17

Eight CD-DA tracks are the same 55-second piece, byte for byte. In
Beetle it plays under the story's first scene; perhaps each chapter of
the story names its own track, and the eight were filled with one piece
(inferred).
